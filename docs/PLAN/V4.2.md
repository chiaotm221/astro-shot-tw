# V4.2 Plan — Light Pollution and Night-Sky Quality

## Goal

Help users compare the bundled observing locations while keeping historical modeled light pollution distinct from live conditions.

## Phase 1 Delivery

- Show an estimated Bortle range, zenith sky-brightness range, naked-eye limiting magnitude, and visible-star range.
- Describe the dominant direction of urban light domes.
- Combine the static site-darkness estimate with simulated Moon illumination and altitude.
- Compare and select all bundled sites in one compact list.
- Avoid assigning fabricated estimates to custom locations.

## Sources and Limitations

The conceptual baseline is Falchi et al., “The New World Atlas of Artificial Night Sky Brightness,” Science Advances 2(6), 2016, DOI 10.1126/sciadv.1600377. The Bortle framework follows John E. Bortle’s 2001 Sky & Telescope scale.

Phase 1 does not directly sample the World Atlas raster at each coordinate. Values are deliberately broad site-level historical planning ranges derived from the existing urban/highland/coast classifications. They are not current satellite measurements, SQM readings, or authoritative Bortle classifications.

## Follow-up

- Sample a redistributable dated raster with documented processing and coordinate resolution.
- Accept optional on-site SQM measurements without overwriting historical source data.
- Combine live weather only at presentation time, keeping weather and light-pollution provenance separate.
