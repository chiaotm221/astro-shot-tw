# V7.0 — Validated Solar-System Ephemerides

## Status

Phase 1 implemented. The shared offline position/event API, dynamic Moon and
planet search/recommendations, information-card events, and the first six-body
JPL Horizons fixture are complete. The full date/location/event fixture matrix,
renderer integration, and browser verification remain pending.

## Objective

Replace deferred or low-precision solar-system behavior with one validated,
documented calculation layer suitable for offline observing and photography
planning. V7.0 is not intended for telescope pointing, occultation prediction,
navigation, or professional astrometry.

## Supported Bodies and Dates

- Moon (JPL target 301)
- Mercury (199)
- Venus (299)
- Mars (499)
- Jupiter (599; fixture generation must record the returned target identity)
- Saturn (699; fixture generation must record the returned target identity)
- Contracted date range: 2020-01-01T00:00:00Z through
  2040-12-31T23:59:59Z
- Dates outside that range may be calculated only after a later validation
  expansion and must not be presented with the V7.0 accuracy claim.

The deliberately narrow date range covers practical trip planning while
keeping the fixture matrix, time-scale assumptions, and approximation errors
auditable.

## Normative Reference

JPL Horizons Observer Tables are the acceptance oracle. Fixtures must be
generated through the official Horizons API and committed to the repository;
production code must never call Horizons and must remain fully offline.

Reference configuration:

- `EPHEM_TYPE=OBSERVER`
- `CENTER=coord@399`, `COORD_TYPE=GEODETIC`
- `SITE_COORD=longitude,latitude,elevation-km`, using east-positive longitude
- `TIME_TYPE=UT`, `TIME_ZONE=+00:00`, `CAL_TYPE=GREGORIAN`
- `REF_SYSTEM=ICRF`, `ANG_FORMAT=DEG`, `EXTRA_PREC=YES`
- Position table: `QUANTITIES=2,4,10`, `APPARENT=AIRLESS`
- Event table: `R_T_S_ONLY=YES`, one-minute search resolution, with Horizons'
  true visual horizon convention
- No daylight, elevation, airmass, elongation, or hour-angle filtering

Horizons quantity 2 supplies apparent RA/declination, quantity 4 supplies
topocentric apparent azimuth/elevation, and quantity 10 supplies illuminated
fraction. Horizons defines apparent Earth-site coordinates as including
light-time, gravitational deflection, stellar aberration, and precession-
nutation to the true equator and equinox of date. Airless output excludes the
optional atmospheric-refraction adjustment.

## Coordinate and Time Contract

Public calculation results must label coordinate semantics explicitly:

- `apparentEquatorialOfDate`: apparent topocentric RA and declination referred
  to the true equator and equinox of date; radians internally.
- `topocentricHorizontalAirless`: azimuth clockwise from true north and
  elevation from the astronomical horizon; degrees at the public boundary.
- `illuminationFraction`: closed interval 0–1.
- `rise`, `transit`, and `set`: UTC epoch milliseconds, or `null` when the event
  does not occur within the requested local civil day.

Inputs use an absolute UTC instant, WGS84-style geodetic latitude/longitude,
and observer elevation in meters. UI time zones affect civil-day selection and
formatting only; they must not alter the underlying instant. Production APIs
must not silently mix J2000 catalog coordinates, astrometric ICRF coordinates,
apparent-of-date coordinates, or refracted elevation.

## Horizon and Refraction Policy

- Direction and rendering use airless topocentric azimuth/elevation because
  this is deterministic and independent of unknown weather.
- Rise and set are user-facing visual events and use the standard apparent
  horizon convention represented by Horizons RETS true-visual-horizon output.
- Transit is the instant the body's center crosses the observer's meridian; it
  is not assumed to be halfway between rise and set.
- Terrain, buildings, local horizon profiles, unusual refraction, and current
  pressure/temperature are excluded and must be disclosed in the UI.
- Near-horizon comparisons use elevation error rather than a combined
  azimuth/elevation separation because azimuth becomes unstable near zenith and
  can be misleading close to singular geometries.

## Accuracy Budget

All limits are maximum absolute error against the committed Horizons fixtures,
not averages.

| Quantity | Moon | Planets | Conditions |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Apparent sky separation from RA/Dec | 0.10° | 0.05° | Entire supported range |
| Airless elevation | 0.15° | 0.10° | Reference elevation at least 5° |
| Airless azimuth | 0.20° | 0.10° | Elevation 5°–85° |
| Illumination | 2 percentage points | 2 percentage points | When provided |
| Rise/set time | 10 minutes | 5 minutes | Event exists in both results |
| Transit time | 5 minutes | 5 minutes | Event exists in both results |

Additional pass rules:

- Event existence must agree exactly: a missing, extra, or wrong-day event is a
  failure even if nearby timestamps would satisfy the numeric tolerance.
- Position rows below 5° elevation are reported separately and must remain
  within 0.25° elevation error; they do not relax the rise/set limits.
- Rows above 85° elevation use angular sky separation and elevation only; raw
  azimuth is excluded because it is ill-conditioned near the zenith.
- RA wrap at 0/360° and azimuth wrap must use shortest-angle differences.
- No supported body, location, or date bucket may be hidden by an aggregate
  mean or percentile.

These are product-planning tolerances, not statements about Horizons' own
accuracy. The wider Moon limits account for its large topocentric parallax and
the greater implementation risk of a compact offline lunar model.

## Fixture Matrix

Fixtures live under `tests/fixtures/ephemeris/v7/` and include raw reference
values plus provenance metadata. The minimum matrix is:

- Bodies: all six supported targets.
- Locations: Taipei, Hehuanshan, Kenting, one zero-elevation equatorial point,
  and one southern-hemisphere point.
- Elevations: actual bundled-site elevation plus an explicit sea-level case.
- Date buckets: 2020, 2024 leap day, 2026, 2030, 2035, and 2040.
- Seasonal coverage: near both solstices and both equinoxes.
- Time coverage per position day: 00:00, 06:00, 12:00, and 18:00 UTC, plus
  samples within 30 minutes of rise, transit, and set.
- Edge cases: UTC/local-day boundaries, RA wrap, azimuth wrap, elevation near
  0°, elevation above 85°, conjunction/opposition windows where applicable,
  and days with no rise or no set.

Each fixture file records:

- Retrieval timestamp in UTC
- Complete encoded Horizons request URL or canonical parameter object
- Horizons target ID and returned target name
- Observer longitude, latitude, elevation, and time zone used for civil-day
  grouping
- Output coordinate/refraction mode and requested quantities
- Raw result rows retained verbatim or a SHA-256 digest plus a lossless parsed
  representation
- Parser/schema version and source attribution

Fixtures are immutable test inputs. Refreshing them requires an explicit review
that separates reference-service changes from implementation changes.

## Secondary Cross-Check

USNO definitions are normative for the product explanation of horizon, rise,
set, and transit, and selected event fixtures should be cross-checked with the
USNO Major Solar System Bodies and Bright Stars service. A disagreement between
USNO and Horizons must be investigated as a definition/configuration issue; the
test must not simply choose the closer value.

USNO notes that actual rise/set observations can differ by a minute or more
because atmospheric refraction is unpredictable, with larger effects from
terrain, observer height, and high-latitude geometry. Therefore the interface
must describe computed event times as planning estimates.

## Test Layers

1. Pure unit tests cover time scales, angle normalization, coordinate frames,
   parallax, interpolation, and event root finding.
2. Fixture tests compare every matrix row and report body/location/date buckets
   independently.
3. Invariant tests enforce finite values, valid ranges, chronological event
   ordering, and continuity away from wrap boundaries.
4. Integration tests confirm Tonight's Sky, search, information cards, camera
   planning, and export consume the same result for one place and instant.
5. Browser tests confirm time-zone formatting and offline operation without
   making a network request.

## Failure Reporting

The fixture runner must print the body, observer, UTC instant, quantity,
expected value, actual value, signed error, and applicable tolerance. It must
also summarize maximum error by body and quantity. Updating a tolerance to make
a failing implementation pass requires a plan-document change with rationale;
fixtures or assertions must not be silently weakened.

## Dependency Decision Gate

Before formulas are implemented, compare candidate approaches using the exact
fixture matrix:

- Accuracy against every budget above
- Minified and compressed client bundle cost
- License and required attribution
- Offline behavior and deterministic versioning
- Tree-shaking and worker compatibility
- Maintenance activity and documented supported date range

A dependency is accepted only if it materially reduces correctness risk and
the measured bundle cost is recorded. Otherwise use focused pure modules with
source citations. No dependency may be selected from popularity alone.

### Phase 1 Decision

Astronomy Engine 2.1.19 is accepted and pinned for the first implementation:

- MIT license and zero transitive runtime dependencies.
- Browser and Node support with built-in TypeScript declarations.
- Upstream algorithms are based on truncated VSOP87/NOVAS-oriented models and
  are independently tested by the project against JPL Horizons and NOVAS.
- The installed upstream browser artifact measures 116,424 bytes minified and
  46,706 bytes gzip. The generated AstroShot client chunk containing the engine
  and other application code measures 236,597 bytes and 83,285 bytes gzip in
  the Phase 1 build; this is not a pure incremental measurement.
- The first committed Taipei fixture at 2026-01-01T12:00:00Z covers the Moon,
  Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, and Saturn. All six pass the V7.0 sky,
  azimuth, elevation, and illumination budgets.

This decision must be revisited if the full fixture matrix exposes a tolerance
failure or if bundle splitting becomes necessary for startup performance.

## Phase 1 Implementation

- `app/simulation/solar-system-ephemeris.ts` provides explicitly named
  apparent-of-date equatorial and airless topocentric horizontal coordinates,
  illumination, visual magnitude, and interval-bounded rise/transit/set times.
- Calls outside 2020–2040 fail the accuracy contract instead of silently
  claiming validated precision.
- Object search resolves Moon and planet coordinates at the simulation instant.
- Tonight's Sky includes dynamically calculated Moon and planet candidates.
- Object information updates dynamic coordinates and displays daily rise,
  transit, and set events with the terrain limitation.
- `tests/fixtures/ephemeris/v7/` stores the first provenance-bearing JPL
  observer fixture.

## Acceptance Criteria

- Every supported body passes every applicable tolerance across the full
  fixture matrix and event-existence checks.
- The supported range and approximation labels are visible in the product.
- Recommendation, search, information-card, camera, and export views agree for
  identical inputs.
- All production calculations remain available offline.
- Fixture provenance and the comparison report are reviewable without calling
  an external service.
- No code path presents V7.0 accuracy claims outside the validated range.

## Deferred

- Dates outside 2020–2040.
- Uranus, Neptune, dwarf planets, satellites, comets, asteroids, and
  user-supplied orbital elements.
- Live Earth-orientation corrections, terrain horizons, and weather-dependent
  refraction.
- Telescope control, plate solving, navigation, and professional astrometry.
